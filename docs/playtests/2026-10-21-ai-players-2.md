# AI players, second session (21 Oct 2026)

The same set-up as the [first session](2026-10-21-ai-players.md): five 3-player matches, each with two AI players
(each with a personality) and one scripted bot. This session ran after the first session's changes:
- Scorch now costs 3 mana per power that burns.
- Spells can use what you attune or summon that round.
- The attack preview counts your own spell.

The notes and logs are in [2026-10-21-ai-players-2/](2026-10-21-ai-players-2/).

## What changed since the first session

- **Scorch is strong but no longer free.**
  - It was cast 18 times, from 0 to 54 mana.
  - It still decided fights: it cancelled attacks, and in one match it saved the win in the last round.
  - Players now weigh it. "Scorching 1 Imp for 3 mana changed nothing." "2 Golems for 18 was poor value."
  - Paying only for what burns was noticed and liked ("a nice rule").
  - The big Scorches were late dumps of mana that had nothing else to buy. 54 mana for 6 Golems "was a free win".
- **Spells on what you summon:** Leyline plus an attack in the same round, on units summoned that round, gave one
  player two conquests.
- **Previews:** "Turn order and the check previews were clear" (match 5). No player complained about the preview
  this time.
- **Ward was used more** (11 casts) and held tiles in four of the notes.

## What they found

- **A free trait point (a bug, fixed in this change).**
  - A Scorch of 1 on a tile holding only Golems was accepted, burned nothing and cost 0, yet it counted toward Hexer
    and Spellweaver. Two Scorches gave 2 trait points for nothing.
  - Now a Scorch too small to burn anything is refused, and a Scorch that ends up burning nothing (its units moved
    away) doesn't count as a cast for the traits.
- **Scorch can't be aimed at an empty tile** (4 notes). The rules say spells "see the round as it will be", but
  that covers only your own attunes and summons, not a rival's secret ones. Players wanted to Scorch the defenders
  a rival might summon there.
- **Mana still piles up:** 66, 44 and 27 left at the end. "Banked mana scores nothing, so hoarding has no payoff."
- **Attacks on empty tiles dominate.**
  - A single big Imp stack took an undefended tile almost every round (match 1).
  - Defenders summoning that same round are the only counter, and attackers who forgot that lost their stacks
    (3 notes).
- **Blight is poor value** (3 notes): 4 mana to cost a rival 1–2 mana of income.
- **Trait goals:**
  - Spellweaver reaches its cap by round 4 and then stops shaping play.
  - Wanderer scored 0 twice: every conquest lands next to your own tiles.
  - Weaver's 9 tiles "felt out of reach" on a 19-tile map.
- **The opening:** passing or losing a bid still costs nothing, and a tied tile can go free to a tied bidder. The
  owner chose to leave this for a playtest with people.
- **Smaller points:**
  - The attack preview repeated the guard: "(guarded by power 2) (guarded by monsters, power 2)". Fixed.
  - A Leyline from a tile that units moved into this round is refused. The refusal now says why: units that step
    can't ride a Leyline too.
  - A Golem that loses 2 power in a battle survives whole. The tool's rules now say so.

## Worth deciding

1. **Scorch on an empty tile:** allow it, burning whatever the rival summons there this round and charging only for
   what burns, or keep the refusal.
2. **Spare mana:** something to spend late mana on. This finding came up in both sessions.
3. **Blight:** make it cheaper or stronger (for example 3 mana, or the tile yields nothing for two rounds).
4. **Trait goals:** retune Spellweaver (cap later, for example 1 point per two different spells), Wanderer and
   Weaver.
