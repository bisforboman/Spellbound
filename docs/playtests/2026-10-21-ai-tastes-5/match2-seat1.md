# Bluffer (match 2, Seat 1)

**Final scores and my trait** Seat 3 (bot, Warbrand) 32, Seat 2 (Wildheart) 25, Seat 1 17. I was Weaver: I finished with a largest group of 1 tile, so 0 points. 1 of my 2 trait guesses was right.

**What I enjoyed**
- Round 2: Seat 2 and I both attuned (1,1) and bounced off each other. I went back for it and Seat 2 backed off. That was a real game of chicken.
- Round 3: I read that Seat 2 would hit (1,1) and summoned 3 Golems there. Their rallied attack (8 against 9) broke on it. That was the best moment of the match.
- Round 4: I counterattacked (2,0), and Seat 2 had read me too and put down 7 Imps. Being outguessed felt fair and fun.
- Trait guessing gave me a reason to watch my rivals. Seat 2's run of Wild tiles gave its trait away, so I guessed Wildheart and got it right.
- The `check` preview and the battle lines are very clear.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- The bot's all-Steady opening looked like Hearthkeeper, but it was Warbrand. Its tells said nothing about its trait, so my guess was a coin flip.
- The bot's single stack of 4 Golems took one empty tile of mine each round. Defending one tile only pushed it to the next empty one. There is no bluff to make against a stack that simply picks the weakest target.
- Every turn I had to choose between growing and guarding. Units protect only their own tile, so with spread-out land the attacker always finds a hole.
- Weaver was hopeless once I was under attack: each lost tile split my group, and I scored 0.
- Fog was thin. I could see every unit next to my land, so "units unseen" only ever hid things far away.

**Did reading my rivals matter?** Yes, against the other AI. Rounds 2 to 4 were pure outguessing, and the read in round 3 was worth a whole army. Against the bot, reading meant nothing: it never bluffed and never reacted to threats.

**Did I have enough to do each round, and did my mana have a use?** Mostly yes. Attune, summon, attack and a spell gave real choices. Mana was tight early and loose in the middle (21 by round 5), and then Golem upkeep (6 a round) drained it to 1. Rally was the spell everyone cast. Lure and Ward rarely beat it.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting decided the match. The leader scored mostly from conquest and its trait. I never built: 5 mana for a Tower worth +2 does not stop a 14-power stack, so it felt like a trap.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
No talk, deals or promises, so there was no way to betray anyone. I couldn't set the two AIs on each other. Threats are seen only after they land.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. Open table talk plus a non-binding pact each round, like Diplomacy's negotiation phase.
2. A Coup-style claim: declare a trait or an action publicly and be challenged on it, with a cost for a false claim.
3. Cosmic Encounter-style allies: invite a rival to add power to your attack for a share of the spoils.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. The early bump-and-read rounds were great. After that, a non-bluffing bot ground my tiles down, and the game lacks the social layer I come for.
