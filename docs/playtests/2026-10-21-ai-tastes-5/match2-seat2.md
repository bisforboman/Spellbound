# Casual player (match 2, Seat 2)
**Final scores and my trait** Seat 3 32, Seat 2 25, Seat 1 17. Seat 2 was Wildheart (3 Wild tiles at the end, full 3 points); both my trait guesses were wrong.

**What I enjoyed**
- Clearing monster tiles. Two Imps plus Rally against a power-3 monster was a cheap, safe win, and "takes it, +2 for conquest" felt like placing a good Carcassonne tile.
- `check` tells me "you win and take it, keeping 3 Imps" before I commit. I never had to do the battle sums, so turns were quick.
- Banking points every round, so a bad round never wiped me out.
- Wildheart was a goal I could hold in my head ("collect Wild tiles"), and it gave me something to steer toward.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- Round 1 I tried to attune the juicy Wild 4 and so did Seat 1, so nobody got it. It was a whole round wasted, with nothing to show for it.
- Round 3 my 2 Golems plus Rally attacked an empty-looking tile and lost 8 to 9 to Golems summoned that same round. `check` said "you win", and I lost my whole army. It felt like a coin flip, not a decision.
- The rules text is long: rally, ward, leyline, blight, lure, reinforce, upkeep, scorch. I only ever used Rally and Reinforce.
- Typing hex coordinates and working out which tiles are neighbours from a list is the opposite of glancing at a Catan board.
- The final round traded tiles back and forth (I took (0,1), Seat 1 took my (1,1) back), which felt random.

**Did reading my rivals matter?** A little. I guessed Seat 1 was Hexer because it kept casting Blight, but it was a Weaver; Seat 3 was a Warbrand, not a Hexer. The guesses felt like noise, and I got zero. Watching where Golems sat (Seat 3's stack at (0,0)) did shape where I put Imps.

**Did I have enough to do each round, and did my mana have a use?** Yes, there was always an attack or a summon. Mana mostly went to Imps, Rally and reinforcements; I ended with 8 unspent, and I never built anything because 5 mana for a Shrine felt slow in an 8-round game.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting monsters, yes, every time. Fighting players was swingy: hidden same-round summons punished me early, then empty tiles fell over late. I never built anything.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
A board I can see at a glance, trading or any friendly interaction, and a clear "big moment" like finishing a long road. The tie rule (nobody gets it) feels stingier than Ticket to Ride, where the first to claim a route keeps it.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. A drawn map with my neighbours highlighted, like the Carcassonne table you can read at a glance.
2. When two players attune the same tile, a tiebreak (turn order or highest bid) instead of nobody, like first-come route claims in Ticket to Ride.
3. Some trade or deal between players, like Catan's "anyone have wood?", so rivals aren't only targets.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. Monster clearing and `check` made it quick and satisfying. But the hidden-summon losses, the coordinates and the long spell list are more than I want on a casual night.
