# Bluffer (match 2, Seat 1)
**Final scores and my trait** Seat 1 41, Seat 3 24, Seat 2 16. My trait was Warbrand, and it was maxed (5 rival tiles taken). My trait guesses: 1 of 2 right.

**What I enjoyed**
- Round 5: Seat 2 saw my rally coming and turned their empty Steady tile into a trap (3 Golems + Ward, 12 against my 11). It was the best moment of the match because someone outguessed me.
- Seat 2 hit back right away. They walked a Golem into my empty (2,-1) while I was summoning. Trading punches felt like Diplomacy.
- Reading traits from the auction worked. Seat 2 tied my bid on a Steady tile and then took two more Steady tiles, so I guessed Hearthkeeper in round 1 and was right.
- Follow orders let me profit from other players' choices: a free-rider Attune in round 1 and a Shrine in round 4.
- The opening tie rule ("a tie wins nobody the tile") turned the auction into a guessing game. I tied with Seat 2 at 3 and still got the tile free.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- The leader runs away. After two easy conquests in rounds 2–3, I had the most mana for the rest of the game. By round 8, a 27-mana Scorch simply erased a defence.
- Warbrand was maxed by round 4. After that my secret goal had nothing left to pull on.
- Players can't talk. A Bluffer has no way to threaten, offer, or lie. The only bluff I had was where I put my units.
- Seat 3 (the bot) cast spells every round, so I guessed Spellweaver. It was Golemwright and ended with 0 Golems. Its play didn't match its trait, so reading it gave me nothing.
- Early on, undefended tiles were free points. Rivals didn't garrison until they had been hit.

**Did reading my rivals matter?** Yes, against Seat 2. The trait read was worth a point, and Seat 2's read of me cost me 3 Golems. Against the bot, reading meant nothing. Fog of war helped: units are only visible next to your own land, so each attack was a real bet on what the defender would summon or Ward.

**Did you follow rivals' stances, and did it change your choices?** I used a follow in rounds 1, 4 and 6. It worked twice (Attune, Build) and fizzled once, at no cost. I picked the followed stance by guessing what the rivals would pick: the bot attunes and builds a lot. It shaped how I spent mana, but it never changed my main stance.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting was very worth it. Conquest gave about 2 points a round and drove my trait. Building mostly came in through follows. I stopped at 3 upgrades, and Tower plus Shrine paid off. A Tower I captured from Seat 2 was a nice bonus.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing** Table talk and binding or non-binding deals (Diplomacy, Cosmic Encounter). There is no way to bluff a hand (Coup). Nothing reins in the leader, so the two players behind me never had a reason to team up against me.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. A one-line public "declaration" each round that can be a lie, like a Coup claim. Rivals could call it out for a cost.
2. Alliances or shared attacks, like Cosmic Encounter allies: invite a rival into your attack and split the reward.
3. Diplomacy-style support orders: back another player's attack or defence with your units, so a team-up against the leader is possible.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. The ambush and counter-punch with Seat 2 were real mind games. But with no talk and a runaway leader, I won alone and nobody could organise to stop me.
