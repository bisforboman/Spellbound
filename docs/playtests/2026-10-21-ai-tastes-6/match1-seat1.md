# Wargamer (match 1, Seat 1)

**Final scores and my trait:** Seat 2 30, Seat 1 29, Seat 3 13. Seat 1 played Warbrand: 7 rival tiles taken, which hit the 3-point cap by round 5. I lost by the single point Seat 2 got for guessing my trait.

**What I enjoyed**
- I attacked in 6 of 8 rounds. The game rewards aggression early: Seat 1 led at 7-5-3 after three rounds just by taking undefended tiles with 3 Golems.
- Rally is a fun card. Its +2 takes the blows first, so my round-4 hit on a Tower plus 3 Imps cost me no Golems.
- Simultaneous moves gave real stories. Seat 3 and I swapped tiles twice in a row. In round 7, Seat 2 and Seat 3 both attacked my empty tile and wiped each other out, so I kept it.
- `check` shows exact power and losses before I commit, which suits my taste.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- Golem upkeep starved me. With 4 Golems I paid 4 of my 4-6 income, and I ended rounds 6-7 with 1 mana. Seat 2 ended with **56 mana unspent** and still won. My army was a tax, and their economy never had to turn into a fight.
- The "battles" are mostly against empty tiles. "Units that attack don't defend" plus one attack per round means everyone hits the tile the other just left. It plays like musical chairs, not a clash of armies.
- Once Warbrand is capped, further conquest is worth only 1-2 banked points. Holding land paid about as well as fighting.
- Losing on a trait guess feels random for a war game.

**Did reading my rivals matter?** Yes, for defence. Seat 3's AI attacked whatever I left empty every round, and I should have warded instead of chasing it. I misread Seat 2 as Beastbane (it lured monsters) when it was Weaver. That cost me the tie, and with it the win.

**Did you follow rivals' stances, and did it change your choices?** Once: in round 3 I followed Summon for a Golem on (-1,-1), and it fired because a rival summoned. It was a cheap hedge but felt like bookkeeping, not strategy. Guessing what others would attack mattered much more than which stance they picked.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting earned points, but the upkeep made a big army a losing trade against a quiet economy. I never built. The Tower I captured gave 1 point and helped defend, which felt good.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing:** a reason to keep big stacks: battles where both armies are present, control of territory that compounds, and an economy that has to be spent.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
- Blood Rage: dying armies score glory, so a lost battle still pays and big fights aren't pure attrition.
- Kemet: a second attack per round (or move+attack chains), so you can strike and then defend.
- Risk: continent-style bonuses for holding a connected region, so conquest compounds instead of plateauing, plus a cap or decay on hoarded mana.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. Rounds are quick, conquest shows up from round 2, and the 1-point loss stings in a good way. But upkeep kept punishing the army and hoarding won.
