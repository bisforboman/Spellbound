# Euro-gamer (match 3, Seat 1)
**Final scores and my trait**: Seat 2 37 (Warden), Seat 1 30, Seat 3 22. Seat 1 was Beastbane and earned only 1 of its 3 points, because the board had just three monster tiles and my rivals cleared the other two.

**What I enjoyed**
- `check` acts as a ledger. Each order showed its exact cost and result before I committed, which suits a careful planner.
- Rally on a single Golem against a power-3 monster cleared it with no losses. Finding small efficiencies like that is the fun I come for.
- Points are banked every round and never lost, so early efficiency kept paying off even after I lost land.
- The rising attune price (1/1/1, then +3) makes "expand or upgrade?" a real choice each round.
- Seat 1's 5-Golem + Ward defence in round 5 wiped out Seat 3's whole army. It was the best moment of the game, because I read the threat and beat it with some arithmetic.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- Luck decided the opening. A tie at the top let Seat 1 take (-2,2) free, and a bid of 2 that tied handed (-2,0) to me at random. That is a swing I did nothing to earn.
- Seat 3 summoned 4 Golems in round 2 and simply took a tile each round. Seat 1 could not match 14 power while also building, so I lost two tiles to an attack I could see coming and could not afford to stop.
- I hardly built anything. Building used my one stance for the round, and the threat and the tempo of conquest kept pulling me away from Shrines. In eight rounds Seat 1 never placed an upgrade (both Towers I ended with came with tiles I took), and my "engine" was whatever land I still held.
- Three monster tiles in total makes Beastbane a race against rivals, not a plan.
- In the last round Seat 2 took (0,1) after I emptied it. An undefended tile is always fair game, so every round becomes a guard-duty tax.

**Did reading my rivals matter?** Partly. I couldn't see rivals' mana, or units away from my borders. I guessed Seat 3 would hit my empty (-2,2) and the guess was right. That one correct read swung about 6 points. Guessing traits was pure noise: I didn't know the names of the traits, so my Weaver and Hexer guesses were shots in the dark.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting paid far better than building. A capture banks 2 points at once and takes the tile's upgrade with it. A Shrine costs 5 mana and returns about 1 mana a round plus 1 point. Every winner's line in the log was "2 for conquest", so the efficient play is to attack.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**: A growth curve. In Terraforming Mars my production compounds. Here income stayed at 6-12 a round and Golem upkeep ate the surplus. There were also too few things to do in one turn.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. Brass-style network bonuses: points when connected upgrades work together, so building compounds.
2. Agricola-style actions: two actions a round, so I could build and defend at once.
3. Terraforming Mars-style public milestones/awards, so goals compete in the open instead of being hidden, guessed traits.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. The ledger and the clean arithmetic of battles are good. But being attacked out of my plan by round 3 is exactly what I avoid, and building never felt like the winning line.
