# Euro-gamer (match 3, Seat 1)
**Final scores and my trait**: Seat 2 37 (Warden), Seat 1 30, Seat 3 22. Seat 1 was Beastbane and earned 1 of its 3 points. There were only three monster tiles, and rivals cleared two of them.

**What I enjoyed**
- `check` shows the exact cost and outcome of an order before I commit, which suits a planner.
- Rally plus one Golem cleared a power-3 monster with no losses, a satisfying efficiency.
- Points are banked every round, so early efficiency still counted after I lost land.
- Seat 1's 5 Golems plus Ward wiped out Seat 3's whole army: I read the threat and beat it with arithmetic.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- Luck decided the opening. Tied top bids meant I got (-2,2) free, and a random leftover gave me (-2,0).
- From round 2 Seat 3 had 4 Golems and took a tile every round. Matching 14 power meant giving up building, so I lost two tiles I could see coming.
- Seat 1 placed no upgrades in eight rounds; one stance a round always went to expanding or defending. My "engine" was just the land I still held.
- Three monster tiles makes Beastbane a race against rivals, not a plan.
- Leaving any tile empty invites a free capture, which works like a guard-duty tax.

**Did reading my rivals matter?** Somewhat. I couldn't see rivals' mana, or units away from my borders. I guessed Seat 3 would hit my empty (-2,2), and that one right read was worth about 6 points. Guessing traits was noise: I didn't know the trait names, and both my guesses were wrong.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting paid far more. A capture banks 2 points at once and takes the tile's upgrade with it. A Shrine costs 5 mana for about 1 mana a round plus 1 point.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**: A growth curve. My income stayed at 6-12 a round, Golem upkeep ate the surplus, and one stance a round left little to plan.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. Brass: bonuses for connected upgrades, so building compounds.
2. Agricola: two actions a round, so I can build and defend at once.
3. Terraforming Mars: public milestones, so goals compete in the open.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. The ledger and the clean battle arithmetic are good, but being attacked out of my plan by round 3 is exactly what I avoid, and building never looked like the winning line.
