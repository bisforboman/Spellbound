# Casual player (match 2, Seat 2)

**Final scores and my trait**: Seat 1 38 (Golemwright), Seat 3 20 (Weaver), Seat 2 15 (Warden, 1 trait point: I held 2 opening tiles). I came last, a long way back.

**What I enjoyed**
- The opening auction was quick and fun. I bid 3 for a tile I liked, won it, and got a free leftover when I lost. Nobody leaves empty-handed, which feels like Carcassonne's gentle start.
- Turns are short: one stance, maybe a spell, done. `check` saying "as things stand, you win" before I commit is lovely.
- Seeing the whole round resolve in a few lines ("Seat 3 takes (-1,-1) with 1 Imp") is a real reveal moment.
- Banking points every round means the score always goes up. Nothing I had banked was taken from me.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- I got punished every time I guessed right but didn't do enough. I put 7 Imps on the tile Seat 3 aimed at, and a surprise Rally (+2) beat me 8 to 7. Then my retake tied 5 to 5 because of a surprise Ward. Then Seat 1 took a tile I'd left empty, twice. Three rounds of my effort came to nothing.
- Every attack lands on whichever tile I left empty, and I can only summon on one tile a round, so a defender can't cover everything. It felt like whack-a-mole where I always lose.
- Ties wiping out both sides feels bad both ways.
- Typing coordinates like "-1,0" and working out hex neighbours from a list is fiddly. I wanted a picture.
- The attune price climbs fast (4, then 7), so growing peacefully stalled after round 2.

**Did reading my rivals matter?** Yes, too much. I couldn't see their mana, or their units unless they were next to me, and Rally and Ward are cast in secret on top. I guessed Seat 3's target correctly in round 3 and still lost because of a hidden +2. My trait guess (Hexer for Seat 1) was a stab in the dark, and so was everyone else's: zero right guesses at the table. Outguessing never decided anything for me. Out-spending did.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting paid for Seat 1: 2 points a conquest, plus Golems feeding its trait. For me it was a mana sink with nothing to show. I never built anything; 5 mana for +1 yield felt slow with 8 rounds. Seat 3's Shrines and Towers earned it 3 points at the end, so building probably was worth it, but nothing told me so mid-game.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**: a board I can look at, trading or any friendly interaction, and a way for the player in last place to catch up. In Catan a bad start still leaves you trading. Here the leader snowballed off my tiles.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. A drawn hex map with my tiles coloured, like the Catan board, instead of a coordinate list.
2. A catch-up rule, like Ticket to Ride's long-route bonus, that gives the trailing player something big to aim for.
3. A friendly action, such as a trade or a pact, like Catan trading, so not every interaction is an attack.

**Would I play again?** 2/5. The turns are quick and clear, but I spent most of the game losing tiles to hidden +2s and never had a satisfying moment of my own.
