# Bluffer (match 1, Seat 2)
**Final scores and my trait**: Seat 1 39, Seat 2 28, Seat 3 17. Seat 2 was Hearthkeeper (Steady tiles) and scored 2 of its 3 trait points.

**What I enjoyed**
- Simultaneous secret orders. Round 6 was a proper read. I bet that Seat 1 would leave its tower tile thin, then scorched it and attacked. Seat 1 had guessed I would hit (-1,1) and warded that tile instead, so I took the tower. Seat 1's warded empty tile showed me exactly what it had been afraid of.
- Bidding on a flashy Shifting tile in the opening to hide that I wanted Steady tiles.
- Ties in the auction and in battle punish predictable play. My 2-mana bid tied Seat 3's, so nobody won the tile, and I got it free anyway.
- `check` shows you what will happen ("you pay less if units move away first"). That lets you plan around what a rival might do.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- I could never guess a trait, because nothing showed me the trait names. A made-up name crashes the tool. Seat 1 guessed "Hexer" straight from the example in the rules and got a free point.
- Seat 3 (the bot) answered my first attack with 6 Imps and a Ward that matched my power exactly. It came out a tie and wiped out my whole army. It felt less like being outguessed and more like the bot always guards the tile you are standing next to.
- Rounds 7 and 8 were a game of swapping empty tiles. Everyone attacked out, nobody defended, and each conquest was worth 2 points. Defending is never worth it in the last rounds.
- There's no way to talk. With three players and one runaway leader, I wanted to say to Seat 3, "you hit Seat 1, I won't touch you." That couldn't happen.

**Did reading my rivals matter?**
Yes, more than I expected, but only through military moves. I couldn't see units away from my border or anyone's mana, so I guessed from what they spent. Seat 3 had warded every round and had to be short on mana, so I attacked it in round 7. The round 6 read on Seat 1 won me a tower tile. A misread in round 2 cost me 3 Golems. Trait reading was dead for Seat 2, because I had no list of names to guess from.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?**
Fighting did: conquest and monsters gave most of my points, and Rally on a monster attack lost me no units, which felt clever. Building I skipped. A Tower changes hands with the tile, and the defender's Ward bonus can't keep up with Rally.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
Talk and deals (Diplomacy), deals you can break (Cosmic Encounter), and claims you can be challenged on (Coup). Rivals matter here, but only through moves on the map. I can't threaten, promise or lie in words.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. Short public messages or non-binding pacts each round (Diplomacy's press).
2. A public list of trait names, plus a way to claim a trait that others can challenge (Coup's claims).
3. One hidden "decoy" order per round, like a fake attack shown to the neighbours you target (Cosmic Encounter's bluff cards).

**Would I play again?** 3/5. The simultaneous attacks give real moments of second-guessing a rival. Without talk, and without trait guessing that works, the bluffing is still thin.
