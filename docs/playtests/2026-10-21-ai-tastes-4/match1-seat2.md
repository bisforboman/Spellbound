# Casual player (match 1, Seat 2)
**Final scores and my trait**: Seat 1 32 (Warbrand, winner), Seat 2 26, Seat 3 23. I was Hearthkeeper (3 Steady tiles at the end, full 3 points).

**What I enjoyed**
- Defending. Twice I had a feeling a rival was coming, so I stacked Golems and a Ward, and both times the report said "power 11 against 12" and then "8 against 15". Those were the best moments of the game, and they were Catan-robber-dodging satisfying.
- Hearthkeeper was a clear goal I could follow without sums: "get three Steady tiles". I always knew what I wanted.
- `check` saying "As things stand, you win and take it" took the maths out of attacks.
- The last two rounds got fun: empty rival tiles next to me, one Golem walks in, points. Ending with 7 tiles felt like growing a Carcassonne city.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- The map is a list of coordinates. I spent more time working out which "(q,r)" touched which than deciding anything. A casual player needs a picture.
- Round 1 had nothing I could attune (everything next to me was still dark), so I just built a Shrine and waited.
- A tie in the opening auction meant nobody won the Steady tile, and then the bot got it for free. That felt like a punishment for making the obvious pick.
- In round 4 I went for a monster tile, and Seat 1 Lured it and attacked it the same round. The two attacks were added together and then fought each other, so I lost my only Golem for nothing. I couldn't have seen it coming.
- I ended with 19 unspent mana and Seat 3 with 38. Mana piles up late and there is nothing satisfying to spend it on.

**Did reading my rivals matter?** A little. Seat 1's three Golems parked next to my Shrine told me plainly what was coming, so I guessed the attack right and that saved my tile. I couldn't see any units away from my border or anyone's mana. I guessed Seat 1 was Warbrand (right, 1 point) and Seat 3 was Spellweaver because it cast Rally every round (wrong, it was Warden). The trait guesses felt like a coin flip worth one point, and they decided nothing. Reading the troops at the border mattered much more.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting, yes, once I had Golems. Defending and grabbing empty tiles was where all my fun and most of my points came from. Building felt weak: 5 mana for one Shrine was a whole turn and gave a small payoff, and I only built once. Golem upkeep was fine.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**: a board you can see at a glance, trading or any friendly contact with other players, and a "big turn" feeling. In Ticket to Ride you finish a long route; here most turns were "summon and wait".

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. A drawn hex map with colours (Catan): seeing the board would cut my thinking time in half.
2. Something to trade or spend late mana on (Catan trading, or Ticket to Ride's end-of-game route bonuses), so a pile of mana isn't wasted.
3. Visible goal cards (Ticket to Ride destination tickets) shown as a progress bar, e.g. "Steady tiles 2/3".

**Would I play again?** 3/5. The defend-and-pounce moments were really good and a game is short, but the coordinate map and the stretches of waiting made it feel like homework.
