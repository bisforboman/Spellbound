# Bluffer (match 2, Seat 1)
**Final scores and my trait**: Seat 2 31 (Spellweaver), Seat 1 26, Seat 3 26 (Warden). Seat 1 was Weaver and banked 2 trait points from one group of 6 tiles. Both of Seat 1's guesses were wrong.

**What I enjoyed**
- The double-blind tie in the first auction: Seat 2 and Seat 1 both bid 4 on the Wild tile, and Seat 2 still ended up with it as the free leftover. That was a cruel little twist.
- Round 5 was a real duel of guesses. Seat 1 sent 3 Golems at an empty (1,0), and Seat 2 had leylined in exactly 3 Golems, so it was a tie and both sides were wiped out. Being read that well hurt, and it was great.
- Round 6 went my way: Seat 1 stacked 6 Golems on (0,1) and scorched (0,0) ahead of the rallied attack, and Seat 2's strike broke on it.
- Because traits are secret, every build and spell is a clue. "No one was offered your traits" is a neat deduction tool: Seat 2 grabbed Wilds, and Seat 1 knew that wasn't Wildheart.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- Seat 2's 27-mana Scorch wiped out all 3 of Seat 1's Golems before the attack happened. Spells resolve before attacks and enemy mana is hidden, so there was no way to see it coming. It felt like being deleted rather than outguessed.
- The attune cost rises steeply (13 mana for a 7th tile), so expansion stalled by round 3. After that, the mid-game for Seat 1 was mostly Ward or hold.
- There is no way to talk, deal or threaten. Seat 3 was a passive tower builder until its last-round strike, and it was impossible to point it at the leader.

**Did reading my rivals matter?** Seat 1 could not see rival mana, units off the border, or summons that hadn't been revealed yet. I guessed Seat 2 was Golemwright because it summoned 7 Golems; it was really Spellweaver, and Ward, Blight, Leyline, Rally and Scorch were all there in hindsight. I guessed Seat 3 was Hearthkeeper because it bid on Steady first; it was really Warden, and its towers on its opening tiles were the tell. I had considered both right answers and then talked myself out of them. That is the kind of mistake I like to make. Outguessing decided two swings: Seat 2's leyline read and Seat 1's defence in round 6. But the winning margin came from Seat 2's spell variety and hidden mana, not from a bluff.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting cost far more than it returned. Seat 1 spent about 30 mana on Golems and gained no tile, and every battle ended in a tie or a wipe-out. A building is a flat 1 point for 5 mana, which beat everything else in the last round. Clearing a monster with a tie (Seat 2's 1 Golem) was a bargain.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**: talk. Diplomacy and Cosmic Encounter come alive through promises and betrayal; here the only social act is a silent guess. Coup's bluff is something you claim and can be called on; here traits are only inferred.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. Non-binding messages or public pacts between rounds (Diplomacy).
2. Inviting a rival to join an attack for a share of the spoils (Cosmic Encounter alliances).
3. A way to reveal or fake a hint about your trait or stance, and to call someone's bluff for a cost (Coup).

**Would I play again?** 3/5. The simultaneous secret orders and trait-reading have real tension. But with no table talk, the "rivals" mostly act like weather.
