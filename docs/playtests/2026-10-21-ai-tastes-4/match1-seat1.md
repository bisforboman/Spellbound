# Wargamer (match 1, Seat 1)
**Final scores and my trait** Seat 1 32 (won), Seat 2 26, Seat 3 23. Trait: Warbrand (4 rival tiles taken, capped at 3 points).

**What I enjoyed**
- Round 2 was a real battle: Seat 1 attacked with 3 Golems plus Rally (11), Seat 2 had secretly summoned 3 Golems and Warded (12). I lost by one point. That was painful and great, like guessing wrong in a Kemet combat.
- The leyline plus attack swing in round 5: I massed Golems on my Wild tile and struck Seat 3's monster-cleared tile while its Imps were away attacking Seat 2. I took it for free.
- Captured upgrades pass to you. Seat 3 built a Tower and a Ward on each tile I threatened, I took both anyway, and kept the Towers for 2 end points. Conquest paid twice.
- Two attackers on one monster tile fought each other (round 4). Seat 1 won and Seat 2's Golem died.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- One stance per round. I can never summon and attack in the same turn, so every offensive costs a "build-up" round, and I ended with 17-20 mana I could not spend. Seat 3 finished with 38 unspent.
- Battles are tiny. Most were 2-3 Golems hitting an empty tile with a Tower and a Ward on it. Losses get decided by spell arithmetic, not armies.
- Undefended tiles everywhere. Seat 2 and I kept trading empty tiles behind each other's backs (my (0,1), (0,0) and (-1,2)). It felt more like whack-a-mole than holding a front.
- The open map: free tiles sat unclaimed all game while we fought.

**Did reading my rivals matter?** Yes, but I mostly read them wrong. Fog meant I saw Seat 2's tile empty in round 2 and missed the 3 Golems plus Ward they summoned in reply. Bots also seem to Ward any tile with my units next to it, and I learned to send just enough power. Both trait guesses missed: I had Seat 3 as Beastbane (it was Warden) and Seat 2 as Warden (it was Hearthkeeper). Seat 2 read me correctly as Warbrand, which wasn't hard.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting clearly paid: 2 points per defended tile, Warbrand, and captured Towers made up most of my lead. Building my own upgrades never felt worth giving up an attack stance. Losing my first army of 3 Golems hurt, but I got back into the game. The rules reward aggression, which I liked.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing** Big, decisive battles and a front line. There's no reason to pile up a huge army, no army leaving its home to march across the map, and no combat cards or hidden commitment beyond Ward and Rally. The board is small, so it feels like skirmishes, not war.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. Let summon and attack happen in the same round, or add a "march" order (Kemet: recruit, then move and fight).
2. Battle cards or a secret bid of extra power when you commit to a fight (Blood Rage).
3. A bonus for holding a contested central region each round, to force a real front (Risk continents).

**Would I play again?** 4/5. Every round had a fight, conquest won the game, and the near-miss in round 2 stuck with me. I'd want bigger battles and fewer turns spent banking mana.
