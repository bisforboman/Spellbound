# Euro-gamer (match 1, Seat 1)

**Final scores and my trait**
Seat 1 (me, Hearthkeeper) 28, Seat 2 (Beastbane) 20, Seat 3 (Weaver) 20. I won on a steady-tile plan: 3 Steady tiles for the trait, 3 points from 34 unspent mana, and land points banked every round.

**What I enjoyed**
- Banked points that can't be taken away. A lead built in round 2 stayed a lead, and every round paid out a little.
- Attune costs 1 + tiles held, so each expansion was a real "is this worth it?" sum. That's a nice, clean curve.
- Simultaneous orders with `check` showing the exact cost. The ledger feel is good.
- A secret trait with a modest goal pointed me at a plan from the opening.
- The summon-then-leyline combo in round 3. My 6-vs-6 tie wiped out a raid I had guessed was coming, which felt clever.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- One stance a round means growth and defence compete for the same slot. From round 3 on, most of my decisions were about guarding tiles, not building.
- Seat 2 scorched my 4 Imps and took a Steady tile with 3 Imps. I lost a trait tile to an attack, not to a choice I misjudged.
- Nothing compounds. Income only grows by one tile at a time, and there's nothing to invest in. After round 4 my best move was to sit on mana (10 mana = 1 point).
- Monsters never appeared, so Beastbane scored 0. Blight in round 8 hit a "next round" that never came.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
Terraforming Mars has cards that raise production and played tags that feed later cards. Agricola has room upgrades and improvements that turn raw goods into points. Brass has industries you build, flip and link up. Spellbound has income but nothing to spend it on that grows the engine.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. **Tile upgrades** (a "Shrine" on a tile raises its yield or turns it Steady). This gives me an investment curve. It's like production tracks in Terraforming Mars.
2. **Converters**: spend mana and tiles to buy point cards, once per round. That makes a sink with choices rather than "hoard for 1 per 10". It's like the major improvements in Agricola.
3. **Adjacency bonuses**: linked tiles of one type yield more. Where I build would then matter beyond defence. It's like the network links in Brass.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. It's quick and clean, and the banked scoring respects planning. But it played as an arms race, and I'd want a real engine before I chose it over a Euro.
