# Casual player (match 3, Seat 2)

**Final scores and my trait**
Seat 2 (me, Wildheart): 29, the winner. Seat 1 (Beastbane): 20. Seat 3 (Weaver): 12. I had 3 Wild tiles by round 2, so my trait was safe early and I spent the rest of the game growing and defending.

**What I enjoyed**
- Turns are quick: pick one stance, maybe add one spell. `check` showing the cost and what's left is great. No maths in my head.
- I had two real "gotcha!" moments. In round 3 I guessed which tile Seat 3's 7 Imps would hit and summoned 8 Imps there, so they all died. In round 6 I held 12 against 11. Reading the round log line by line was like flipping a card in Catan.
- Scorch is a neat panic button. Wiping 7 Imps before they could attack felt powerful.
- Unveils add a little surprise, like drawing a new Carcassonne tile.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- Finding things on the map. A list of "(q,r)" coordinates plus the neighbour formula means I'm doing hex arithmetic to see what is next to what. It's the fiddly sums I hate.
- Defending is mostly guessing which of three tiles gets hit. When I guess wrong, I just lose it.
- Scoring is murky: 1 point per 3 tile-rounds, 1 per 12 mana, 1 per 10 left over. I never felt a clear "I just scored!" moment, only totals creeping up.
- My scorch refused to target units that were about to be summoned, though the rules say spells "see the round". That only applies to your own units, and it confused me.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
- Ticket to Ride's visible goals: route cards give you a clear plan and a big reveal at the end. Here traits do some of that, but they're worth only 3 points and were done by round 2.
- Carcassonne's spatial joy: you can see your city grow. Here I can't see the board.
- Catan's trading and table talk: nothing passes between players except attacks.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. A drawn hex map with neighbours highlighted. It makes the game readable at a glance, like Carcassonne's tile layout.
2. Bigger, chunkier scoring moments, such as completing a region or a ring of tiles. It gives a satisfying payoff, like Ticket to Ride's routes or Carcassonne's closed cities.
3. Trading or deals between players, such as swapping mana for a non-attack promise. It creates social fun, like Catan's trading.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. It's quick and has real bluff tension, but it needs a picture of the map and juicier scoring to grab me.
