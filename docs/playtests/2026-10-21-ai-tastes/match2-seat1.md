# 4X fan (match 2, Seat 1)

**Final scores and my trait**
Seat 3 (bot, Wildheart) 24, me (Warden) 23, Seat 2 (Hexer) 22. I lost by one point. Seat 3's 2 Imps took my undefended opening Wild tile in round 6, which cost me a Warden point. My round-7 attempt to take it back ended in a three-way fight with no winner.

**What I enjoyed**
- The Unveils are real exploration. Monster tiles showing up in rounds 2 and 4 gave me something to go after.
- Lure followed by a lone Golem was a neat combo. The Golem can't die to 2 damage, so I cleared (0,2) with no losses. Rules that leave room for tricks like that are what I enjoy.
- `check` and the ledger are excellent. I always knew what an order would cost and do.
- Simultaneous orders give you real "did they read me?" moments. The three-way collision at (2,-2) was a good story.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- It's flat. My round 8 felt like my round 1 with more mana: same stance menu, same unit types, same spells. I had 31 mana and nothing interesting to spend it on.
- The map is tiny. By round 5 there was nothing free next to me, so in round 8 I couldn't attune at all.
- One stance per round makes it slow: summon one round, attack the next. In 8 rounds that leaves about 4 meaningful actions.
- Getting blighted three times running felt like a tax, and there was little I could do about it.
- Points come in drips, so the margins are a coin flip (24/23/22).

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
- Civilization: no tech tree or buildings, so there's nothing to invest in for later.
- Eclipse: no ship or unit upgrades and no sectors to discover outward. There is only one Unveil pool.
- Twilight Imperium: no objectives to race for and no diplomacy or trading. There's also no strategy-card choice that changes what you can do each round.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. **Tile improvements** (Civilization): spend mana to build a tower or shrine on a tile that raises its yield or defence. Mana becomes growth, not just leftover points.
2. **A small research track** (Eclipse): buy tiers such as Golem power 4, a second spell per round, or a cheaper attune. That gives the small-to-mighty arc.
3. **Public objectives** revealed each round (Twilight Imperium): goals to race for that pull players across the map. I'd also want the map to grow, with more Unveil rings.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. The core is clever, readable and quick, and the opening auction is spicy. But it's a skirmish, not an empire builder, and I want growth that changes how I play.
