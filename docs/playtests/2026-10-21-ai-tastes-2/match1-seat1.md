# Euro-gamer (match 1, Seat 1)

**Final scores and my trait** Seat 2 46 (Weaver), Seat 1 21, Seat 3 14 (Warbrand). Seat 1 was Hearthkeeper and finished with 3 Steady tiles for the full 3 trait points.

**What I enjoyed**
- `check` is excellent. Seeing the cost and the outcome before committing ("leaving 4", "you win, keeping 2 Golems") is the kind of transparency I want.
- The Hearthkeeper goal shaped my opening bids and my attunes, and it paid out exactly as planned.
- Ward plus 2 Golems tying Seat 3's 3 Golems in round 3 was a satisfying, efficient trade: 7 mana removed their whole army.
- Points banked every round and never lost make an early lead feel earned.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- The cheapest engine was an army. Seat 2 summoned 4 Golems for 8 mana in round 1 and took my tiles in rounds 2 and 3. That gave them land, income and 2 points per tile, while each attune cost me 10 and climbs by 3 for every tile I hold. Growing by attuning cannot keep up with taking land.
- Building helps the aggressor. Upgrades pass to whoever takes the tile, so Seat 3's Shrines mostly ended up as Seat 2's points.
- Defence costs too much: holding a tile against 12 power with Ward and Golems would have cost my whole turn, and I never had a reason to build a Tower.
- Seat 3 Blighted my Wild tile three rounds running. There was nothing I could do back, and it simply erased income.
- Auction ties that give the tile to nobody, plus a random free tile for everyone who lost, felt like luck that decided my start.
- Seat 2 ended with 61 mana unspent and still won easily. The economy outgrew the things there were to spend it on.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Shrines were fine: 5 mana bought +1 income and 1 end point, close to what an attune was worth. But they only paid off on tiles nobody could reach, and few tiles were safe. Attacking was clearly the best return. My one attack, in the last round, scored 2 points for the conquest plus the steady and Shrine points that came with the tile. That was more than any build. In Euro terms the most efficient action was to take someone else's engine, which is the opposite of what I look for.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
Brass and Terraforming Mars let you build an engine that compounds over the game. Here the engine is fragile and hardly grows. There are no cards or tech that change what an action does, and no actions or spaces that two players can compete for without fighting. Interaction comes almost entirely from attacks and Blight.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. Upgrades that chain or synergise, like card tags in Terraforming Mars (for example, a Shrine next to a Sanctum yields more).
2. Brass-style connections: income or points for linking your tiles, so building matters even if a tile might be lost.
3. Agricola-style shared action spaces or a market, so players compete by getting there first rather than by conquest. Make attuning cheaper, or raise the defence bonus, so growing peacefully can keep up.

**Would I play again?** 2/5. The interface and the clear rules are good. But the best strategy was a round 1 Golem rush, and my plan was mostly about not being attacked.
