# Casual player (match 3, Seat 2)

**Final scores and my trait**
Seat 3 won with 32, Seat 1 had 27 and Seat 2 came last with 14. My trait was Golemwright (1 point per 3 Golems at the end). I finished with 0 Golems, so it scored 0.

**What I enjoyed**
- The opening auction is quick and fun. It's one bid and it's done, and a tie that wins nobody the tile made me laugh.
- Every round ends with a short log of what happened, like "Seat 2 takes (1, 0) with 4 Golems". It reads like a little story.
- `check` told me in plain words that I would win and keep 1 Golem. That gave me confidence without any maths.
- Points are banked and never lost, so even a bad round gave me something.
- Retaking (2,0) with my Golems was the best moment of the game.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- Seat 1 Blighted my best tile (1,1) in rounds 1, 2 and 5. My 3-yield tile kept paying out 0. That was the opposite of fun, and I had no way to stop it.
- In round 6, Scorch burned both my Golems before my attack began. My attack just "had no units left to send". I lost my whole army to a spell I never saw coming.
- Golem upkeep slowly crushed me. Income ~3-5 with 4 Golems meant they dissolved. Golemwright pushed me to build Golems, and the economy punished me for doing it.
- Reading the map as a list of "(q,r)" coordinates and working out neighbours with the formula is real homework. It's no Catan board.
- Attune cost 10, then 13, so I could only grow once. After that it was all defence.
- Seat 3 sat on 64 mana and then dropped 32 Imps in the final round. The runaway leader was obvious from round 4 and nobody could catch them.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?**
Fighting paid well in points. Each capture gave +2, which was most of my score. But it was swingy: I'd take a tile and lose it again the next round. Golems felt too expensive to keep against cheap Imps plus Scorch. The upgrades I bought late (a Tower, a Shrine) were a safe 1 point each, but they cost 5 mana. The Tower didn't stop 9 Imps, so it felt like wasted mana. Overall, attacking was the only real way to score, and defending felt hopeless.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
I can't see a picture of the board, the way you can in Carcassonne. There's no trading or catch-up, the way there is in Catan. And there's no clear goal to work towards, the way the route cards give you one in Ticket to Ride. Being singled out by Blight with no answer felt like the robber, but every turn.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. A drawn hex map, even simple ASCII (Carcassonne: I want to see my land grow).
2. A limit on hitting the same player again and again, or a cheap way to cleanse a Blight (Catan's robber moves on; this didn't).
3. Visible goal cards with a clear payoff and a catch-up bonus for the last-place player (Ticket to Ride's tickets).

**Would I play again?** 2/5
The turns are quick and the logs are fun. But I spent most of the game watching my stuff get Blighted, Scorched and taken. My trait was impossible to score, and I couldn't picture the map.
