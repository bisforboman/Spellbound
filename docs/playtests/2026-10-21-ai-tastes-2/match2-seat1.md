# 4X fan (match 2, Seat 1)

**Final scores and my trait**
Seat 2 (Hexer) 27, Seat 3 (Golemwright) 18, Seat 1 (Wildheart) 8. Seat 1 ended with 2 tiles, one of them Wild, so the trait gave 1 point.

**What I enjoyed**
- The Unveils in rounds 2 and 4 felt like exploring for a moment: dark hexes turning into Wild and Shifting tiles I wanted.
- The Shrine was the closest thing to growing an economy: build once, earn more every round.
- Simultaneous orders made for real "did they see it coming" moments. In round 6 Seat 2 and Seat 3 both sent 2 Golems at my empty tile and wiped each other out.
- `check` is excellent. Seeing the cost and the result before committing is what a 4X ledger should be.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- Expansion was effectively closed. Attuning cost 10 mana with 3 tiles while my income was 1 to 5 a round, so Seat 1 never attuned once. Free tiles sat unclaimed all game while I could see them.
- Blight is a snowball against the weakest player. Seat 3 blighted my Shrine tile three rounds running, and Seat 2 blighted my other tile, so my income dropped to 1. I never got off the ground.
- Scorch hard-counters Imps. I paid 4 mana for 4 Imps, and Seat 2 burned all of them before my attack resolved. Two of my attacks fizzled with "no units left to send".
- Round 8 felt like round 1, only poorer. There was no arc from small to mighty, and no tech path beyond three one-off upgrades.
- Wild tiles yielded 0 as often as they paid out. That makes Wildheart a trap when you start with a 0-yield tile.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?**
The Shrine paid back in theory, but it was blighted for most of the game and then conquered, which handed my investment and its end point to Seat 3. For the others, upgrades clearly paid. Seat 2 and Seat 3 each banked 3 points from late Towers and Shrines, a cheap end-game dump of spare mana rather than a real tech tree. Attacking was the only way to expand, since conquest gives a tile plus 2 points while attuning cost a fortune. But a mana-rich player can cast Scorch, which beats any Imp army, so fighting only paid for whoever was already ahead.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
Growth. In Civilization or Eclipse a weak start still compounds: more land means more income, which means more land. Here attune costs rise with every tile you hold, and Blight stops income, so the leader compounds and the trailer is locked out. There is no exploration reward either, such as ruins or discoveries, no research, and no feeling of an empire getting bigger.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. **Cheaper or flat-cost expansion** (Civilization settlers). Attune should cost a flat fee, or scale more gently, so that small realms can grow.
2. **A short tech track** (Eclipse research). Upgrades that unlock better ones in later rounds, so round 8 plays differently from round 1.
3. **Exploration rewards on Unveil** (Twilight Imperium exploration cards). A tile that gives a one-off bonus to whoever claims it first, plus a catch-up limit on Blight, such as not hitting the same tile twice in a row.

**Would I play again?** 2/5. The simultaneous-reveal core is tense and the tooling is clear. But in this match Seat 1 was starved out by round 3 and spent the last five rounds unable to expand or build, which is the opposite of what I play 4X games for.
