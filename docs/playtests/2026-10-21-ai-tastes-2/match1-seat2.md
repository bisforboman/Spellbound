# Wargamer (match 1, Seat 2)
**Final scores and my trait**
Seat 2 46, Seat 1 21, Seat 3 14. Seat 2 won as Weaver (a connected group of 9 tiles, the full 3 points).

**What I enjoyed**
- One early army (4 Golems, round 1) won the game. I made six attacks in rounds 2 to 8 and took a tile with every one, including a monster tile. Each one was worth 2 points, and every capture also grew my income and my land points.
- Leyline plus attack in the same round is a great combo. I sent Golems across my realm and struck the same turn, which felt like a real flanking move.
- Captured Shrines change hands. Taking Seat 3's upgraded Wild tiles felt like looting.
- The `check` preview made committing to an attack easy: power against power and what survives, before I commit.
- Simultaneous orders created real tension. In round 8, Seat 1 took my back tile while I took theirs.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- There were almost no battles. Nearly every attack hit an empty tile, and the only clash was Seat 3's against Seat 1 in round 3 (9 against 9, both wiped out). Defenders used Ward, but I never fought an army I had to outplan.
- Attacking was so lopsided that it became a snowball: 61 mana unspent at the end, and nothing to spend it on, because one stance a round meant I couldn't summon and attack in the same round.
- Scorch can't be cast pre-emptively at a tile that is empty now, so I couldn't insure an attack against defenders summoned that round.
- The last round has no aftermath, so attacking it costs nothing. Seat 1 and I both took a tile there, freely.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?**
Fighting paid off hugely, perhaps too much: a 2-mana Golem that takes a tile earns 2 points and the tile's income. I never built anything. I got two Shrines by conquest, which was more satisfying than paying 5 mana for one. Ward was the best defensive buy at 3 mana, but a Ward alone never stopped a 9-power attack. Seat 3 (Warbrand, the "war" trait) built Shrines and kept getting raided, so the AI seats' economic play lost to aggression by a mile.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
Blood Rage and Kemet have armies that meet and clash, and losing a battle still gives you something. Here defenders rarely field armies, so it plays more like Risk against an empty board. There is no combat hand or card trick to bluff with, no reinforcement mid-fight, and no catch-up for the player being picked apart.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. Battle cards or a secret commitment of bonus power (Kemet / Blood Rage): a chance to outguess the other player, beyond comparing totals.
2. Let a player summon and attack in one round at a premium, or let mana buy reinforcements during a battle (Risk's card turn-in): something to sink a war chest into.
3. A reward for the loser of a battle (Blood Rage sends dead units to Valhalla for glory), so defending and counterattacking are worth trying and the leader can't snowball unopposed.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. The core of moving armies, striking and taking land felt good and was quick. But with no real clashes and nothing able to stop a runaway leader, it was a march rather than a war.
