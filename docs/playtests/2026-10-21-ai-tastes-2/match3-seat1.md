# Bluffer (match 3, Seat 1)

**Final scores and my trait**: Seat 3 (bot, Wildheart) 32, Seat 1 27, Seat 2 (Golemwright) 14. I took Hexer (3 points for Scorch or Blight casts) because it is the trait that makes me interact with the other players.

**What I enjoyed**
- The tied auction gave me (0,0) for free, so tying on purpose looks like a real bluffing tool.
- Simultaneous orders work like Diplomacy. Seat 2 summoned 3 Golems to guard its 3-yield tile, so in round 2 I took the empty (2,0). In round 4 they guarded (1,1) again and I took (2,0) back. Guessing right twice felt great.
- Blight works as a long threat. Three Blights on (1,1) left Seat 2 short on income, and in round 5 two of its Golems dissolved because it couldn't pay upkeep. That felt like a win from hexing rather than fighting.
- The Scorch in round 6 burned both Golems before they could attack (2,0). The log line "Seat 2's attack on (2, 0) has no units left to send" was the best moment of the match.

**What bored or frustrated me**
- The bot (Seat 3) mostly ignored me and piled up mana: 64 in round 8, then 32 Imps it never used. Seat 3 still won with Shrines, a Tower and its trait. Out-reading people mattered less than sitting still.
- There is no table talk: no offers, no threats and no binding deals. Seat 2 and I wore each other down while the third player won, and I couldn't propose a truce or an alliance against the leader.
- You can't summon and attack in the same round. In round 7 I had to choose between a sure summon and a 1-Imp gamble on (1,1). I chose the summon, and (1,1) turned out to be undefended.
- My Hexer goal was full after 3 casts by round 5, so it gave me no reason to hex after that.

**Did building and fighting feel worth it?** Fighting was the only thing that kept the game interesting. Each capture was worth +2, and each one came from out-guessing Seat 2. But the bot's quiet economy plus 3 upgrades (each worth 1 point at the end) beat my 4 captures. My only upgrade point came from the Tower on (1,0), which I captured. Builds are clearly worth their 5 mana, which says the game rewards building more than reading your rivals. Scorch at 3 mana per power is expensive (18 mana for two Golems), but it was decisive.

**Compared with the games I love, what's missing**
Negotiation and betrayal (Diplomacy, Cosmic Encounter), and bluffing about who you are (Coup). Traits are only secret during the opening, which is too short to bluff with.

**Top 3 additions I'd want**
1. A message or proposal step before orders, like Diplomacy's press: a non-binding "I won't attack you" that you can break.
2. Keep traits secret until the end, so players guess, like Coup's hidden roles.
3. A way for players without a fight in it to back one side in a battle, like Cosmic Encounter's allies, so the third player can't sit out.

**Would I play again?** 3/5. The guessing rounds against another person were tense and fun, but too often a player who ignores everyone still wins.
